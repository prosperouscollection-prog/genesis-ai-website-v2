After-Hours Calls: Where Dental Offices Lose Patients

September 05, 2026

A general dental practice in metro Detroit typically brings in $600 to $1,000 in production from a new patient's first visit, and that same patient is often worth $4,000 to $8,000 over the next several years once you count the cleanings and treatment plans that follow. Most practices field a steady share of new-patient calls outside the 8-to-5 window, and a front desk that closes when the doors close sends every one of those calls straight to voicemail.

The front desk closes before the calls stop

A two- or three-chair practice usually runs one or two people at the front desk, and both are busy checking patients in and out for most of the day. Once the last patient leaves and the desk locks up, any call after that goes unanswered. New-patient calls do not follow office hours. Someone with a toothache at 7pm, a parent booking a child's first cleaning after work, or a person who just switched insurance and is searching for a dentist on a Saturday morning are calling when it is convenient for them, not when your staff is at a desk.

What one lost new patient actually costs

A single missed new-patient call is not a small miss. At $600 to $1,000 in first-visit production and $4,000 to $8,000 in patient value over five years, one missed call can equal a week of hygiene appointments in lifetime value. Practices in Livonia, Sterling Heights, or Dearborn compete in a market with dozens of general and family dentists within a short drive, so a caller who cannot reach anyone has other options within minutes, not days.

Voicemail does not hold a new patient's attention

Someone calling a dental office for the first time is usually comparing two or three practices, often from a Google search for "dentist near me accepting new patients" or a referral from a friend. If the call goes to voicemail, most people do not wait for a callback. They hang up and dial the next name on the list, and that practice books the appointment instead. Existing patients tend to leave a message and wait, but new patients rarely do, and new patients are the ones driving practice growth.

Closing the gap without adding staff

Genesis AI Systems answers every call to your practice 24/7, books the appointment on the spot when a patient wants to get on the schedule, takes a message for anything that needs a human follow-up, and texts you a summary right after the call ends. Calls in the evening, on a weekend, or during a busy Tuesday morning all get answered the same way. There is no extra front-desk shift to schedule and no new hire to train.

New-patient inquiries are not the only calls that slip through. Existing patients confirming appointments, insurance questions after the front desk closes, and someone rescheduling a cleaning before a trip all land on the same voicemail box, and each one is a small piece of goodwill that either gets handled right away or waits until the next business day.

For a practice picking up even a few of the new-patient calls it used to lose after hours, the added production from those patients pays for the gap many times over across a year.
