CNA Program

The nurse aide class, start to finish.

What the two schedules look like, what a day is like, what to bring, and what the State of Michigan requires before your name goes on the registry.

Two schedules. Same class.

Both cover the full State of Michigan nurse aide curriculum, 75 hours of classroom, skills lab and clinical time, and both end with the state exam given in our building.

Morning class

Two weeks

Monday to Friday, 8:30 am to 4:00 pm. Ten class days. Good for people who can take two weeks off work or who are between jobs and want the certificate fast.

Evening class

Three weeks

Monday to Friday, 5:00 pm to 10:00 pm. Fifteen class days. Built for people who work days. You keep your job and still finish in under a month.

How the weeks run

Classroom, skills lab, clinical, exam.

The first days are in the classroom and the skills lab at 7 Mile. You cover what a nurse aide does and why: infection control, body mechanics, vital signs, personal care, feeding, positioning and transfers, bed making, and the rights of the people you care for. Every skill gets practiced on the beds and mannequins in the room, then checked off one on one with Ms. Sam.

Then you put in your clinical hours at a nursing facility, in scrubs, with real residents, under supervision. Most students say this is the part that makes it real.

The last days are review. Then you sit for the state exam, written and skills, here at the school. You do not travel to a testing center. When you pass, your name goes on the Michigan Nurse Aide Registry and you can start applying.

Our students pass the state exam at a high rate the first time. Ask about it when you call.

Before you start

What the state and the school require.

  1. A valid ID

    A Michigan driver's license or state ID. Bring it on your first day. High school students are welcome; call us first so we can talk through the schedule with you.

  2. A TB test

    Dated within the last two years. You don't need it to start, but it has to be turned in before you finish the class and go to clinical.

  3. A clean enough record

    Michigan keeps anyone with a felony conviction in the last 15 years off the nurse aide registry, along with certain abuse, neglect, theft or fraud misdemeanors in the last 10 years. If you have something on your record, call before you pay a deposit and we'll go through it with you.

  4. A short interview

    On your first day you sit down with Ms. Sam. She wants to know why you're here and if you're ready to take care of people who can't take care of themselves. That's it.

Dress code and what to bring

Burgundy scrubs, every day.

Students wear burgundy scrubs to class and to clinical. We sell sets at the school for $50, in men's and women's fits, sizes XS to 3XL. If you already own burgundy scrubs, bring them.

Closed shoes only. No Crocs, no sandals, no open backs. You will be on your feet and around beds and equipment.

Bring your ID, a pen and a notebook. The textbook is optional. It's $75, sold at the school, and it helps if you like to study at home, but the class covers everything on the exam.

Common questions

About the class itself.

Can I miss a day?

The state counts hours, and the class is built to hit the required 75 with nothing to spare. Talk to Ms. Sam before you miss anything, so she can work out how you make it up.

Is the clinical at the school?

No. Clinical hours are at a nursing facility, with real residents, under supervision. You'll get the location and the days from Ms. Sam during the first week.

Where do I take the state exam?

Here at 1004 W 7 Mile Rd. The school is a state exam test site, so you test in the room you learned in.

What if I fail the exam?

You can retake it. Ask us about the refresher class, which goes back over the skills the examiners check, before you schedule the retake.

Do you help with jobs?

Yes. We help graduates find placements, and facilities that know our students call us when they're hiring. Many students are hired where they did their clinical hours.
